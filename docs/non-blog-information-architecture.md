# Sourcing Ally non-blog information architecture expansion

## Purpose

The platform already has strong category hubs, dynamic library pages, tools, buyer paths, and Journal content. The navigation currently exposes only three grouped choices, however, and the high-value operational pages are hidden inside hubs or do not yet exist as dedicated decision pages. The expansion should make the site feel like a complete sourcing operating system rather than a small brochure with a blog.

The new structure prioritizes pages that help a buyer decide what to do next. Each page will be written as a buyer-facing guide with a defined use case, practical scope, limitations, related internal links, and a clear next action. It will not rely on invented client results, certifications, or unverified claims.

## Navigation model

| Main navigation area | Existing role | Planned additions |
|---|---|---|
| Work with us | Service engagement and project-start tools | **How we work**, **Fees & scope**, factory visit planning, quality-control planning |
| Learn | Educational libraries and Journal | Supplier verification, product samples, shipment readiness, buyer playbooks |
| Explore China | Product and city discovery | China business travel, industrial-region planning, market and factory-visit connections |

Two direct top-level items have been added because they answer high-intent questions that should not be buried inside a dropdown: **How we work** and **Fees & scope**. The three grouped menus remain available by desktop hover, keyboard focus, or click; mobile uses a click-open Menu control.

## First-page cluster

The first rich page cluster contains eight decision pages. Six localized versions of each page will create 48 meaningful non-blog routes, not duplicate thin pages.

| Route family | Buyer question it answers | Primary navigation placement | Essential internal links |
|---|---|---|---|
| `/how-we-work/` | What happens from the first message through supplier work, checks, and shipment preparation? | Direct top-level link; Work with us | Buyer paths, Project Brief Builder, Service Matcher, services |

| `/supplier-verification/` | What evidence can be checked before an order, and what does each check not prove? | Learn | Questions, supplier sourcing, factory visits, Journal verification guide |
| `/product-samples/` | How should a buyer plan, evaluate, revise, and record a sample before production? | Learn | Product Brief Builder, quality control, product guides, sample articles |

## Content pattern for every decision page

Each dedicated page should be substantial enough to stand alone, not simply repeat a hub card. The base pattern is a clear opening answer, a practical decision map, a scoped checklist, a section on what the activity can and cannot prove, a related-resource route, and a contact or tool next action. Where a topic contains changing legal, shipping, tax, or compliance facts, the page will clearly identify the need for current specialist advice rather than presenting a general guide as legal advice.

A practical competitor review supports this decision-first structure: a factory-audit page is most useful when it distinguishes the buyer’s question, explains what is checked, sets limits, identifies a process, and links to a next action. Sourcing Ally will use that structural lesson without copying content, pricing, or claims. [1]

## Internal-link pathways

The central pathway is not a one-way funnel. A buyer who begins with a city page should be able to reach a factory-visit planner and then supplier verification. A buyer who begins with a product brief should reach samples, quality-control planning, and shipping readiness. A buyer who begins with fees should find the service matcher and an accurate explanation of independent factory travel support.

| Starting point | Main next decision | Supporting routes |
|---|---|---|
| Product idea | Build a usable brief | Product samples → Supplier verification → Quality-control planning |
| Supplier shortlist | Decide what evidence is needed | Supplier verification → Factory visits → Service Matcher |
| Sample approved | Decide what must be controlled before shipment | Quality-control planning → Shipping readiness → Freight-forwarder guide |
| China trip | Turn a visit into a documented sourcing decision | China business travel → China Route Map → Factory visits → city guide |

## Build sequence

The first implementation will publish the shared hover navigation and the two foundation pages, **How we work** and **Fees & scope**, in all six website languages. The next implementation will add the supplier verification, product samples, factory visits, and quality-control planning cluster. Shipping readiness and China business travel then connect the operational and geographic parts of the platform.
