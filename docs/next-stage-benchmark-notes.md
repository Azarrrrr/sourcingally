# Next-stage benchmark notes

The strategic lesson is not to copy competitor pricing or claims. It is to make the buyer’s decision process more visible, specific, and connected.

The U.S. International Trade Administration describes due diligence as a continuing process of evaluating potential partners and risks. For Sourcing Ally, this supports keeping supplier verification, samples, factory visits, and quality-control planning as connected decision pages rather than presenting any one check as a guarantee. [1]

JingSourcing’s factory-audit material distinguishes a buyer’s core question from a generic audit, then lists the evidence considered, the workflow, and a resulting next action. Its inspection content similarly separates inspection scope, sampling, communication, issue follow-up, and the limits of an inspection. This suggests that Sourcing Ally’s next rich pages should use explicit “what this checks / what it does not establish / what you decide next” structures. These are structural observations only; no competitor price, performance, or claims should be reused. [2] [3]
