# Shipping Readiness and China Business Travel Guide Specification

## Objective

Add two decision-oriented page families that take buyers beyond supplier selection and into two practical stages: preparing a shipment and preparing a purposeful China factory trip. The pages must educate without impersonating a freight forwarder, importer of record, legal adviser, immigration adviser, or travel-security authority.

| Page key | Route | Primary visitor question | Role in the platform |
|---|---|---|---|
| `shippingReadiness` | `/[lang]/shipping-readiness/` | “What needs to be clear before goods leave the factory?” | Connects sample approval, quality control, packaging, delivery terms, and forwarder coordination. |
| `chinaBusinessTravel` | `/[lang]/china-business-travel/` | “How do I make a factory trip useful rather than random?” | Connects China route planning, factory visits, language support, and agreed travel scope. |

## Shared page experience

Both families will reuse the established Decision Suite experience: a substantial editorial hero, three situations where the guide helps, an evidence path, explicit boundaries, a preparation checklist, three contextual next routes, and a project-brief call to action. This makes them visually coherent with supplier verification, product samples, factory visits, and quality-control planning while preserving distinct content.

## Content boundaries

The shipping guide should describe how a buyer can prepare a clear handover package for a chosen forwarder and importer. It can discuss a written product and quantity reference, agreed delivery term and named place, packaging and cargo measurements, commercial and packing information, shipment timing, and destination-specific questions. It must state that shipping, customs, duty, import eligibility, insurance, and document requirements depend on the goods, route, and destination and should be confirmed by the buyer’s forwarder and importer.

The business-travel guide should describe pre-arranged factory visits, route design, meetings, product evidence, visit records, payments and connection preparation, and language support. It must explain that entry and transit requirements vary and should be confirmed through relevant official sources, carriers, accommodation, and the traveller’s applicable Chinese embassy or consulate. It must not recommend a visa type or decide an individual’s eligibility.

## Internal-link design

Shipping readiness should lead to the quality-control plan, packaging coordination, and the freight-forwarder Journal guide. Business travel should lead to factory visits, China Route Map, and Fees & scope. All links must preserve the selected language; where an edition does not exist, the route should remain a sensible localized hub page rather than silently direct to English.

## Language and trust requirements

Create English source copy, then produce native, non-literal Spanish, Brazilian Portuguese, Russian, Turkish, and French versions. Preserve scope limitations in every language. Do not make claims about shipment guarantees, customs clearance, visa approvals, real-time route availability, factory capacity, customer results, or regulatory compliance.
