# Sourcing Ally: recommended next stage

## Executive recommendation

The next goal should **not** be to add generic pages simply to make the site larger. Sourcing Ally already has a strong base: 565 static pages, six-language navigation, buyer paths, service pages, product and city libraries, interactive tools, legal pages, and a Journal. The present gap is that the most important sourcing decisions—how to verify a supplier, use a sample, plan a factory visit, and define quality control—are still distributed across hubs, services, Questions, and articles.

The strongest next move is to build a connected **Supplier Decision Suite**. This will create rich, six-language pages that answer the questions a serious buyer has just before sending money, approving a sample, booking a visit, or allowing goods to ship. It gives the website practical depth and stronger internal linking without relying on invented testimonials, client counts, or certifications.

## Current position

| Current strength | What it already achieves | Remaining opportunity |
|---|---|---|
| 565 static pages across six languages | The site already has scale, a visible editorial system, and a broad search footprint. | The number of rich, transaction-adjacent decision pages is still small compared with the product, city, and Journal inventory. |
| Services, Buyer Paths, Questions, product/city/guides libraries | Visitors can research a topic and move into a tool or service area. | A buyer still has to assemble the full decision path across several pages. |
| How we work and Fees & scope | The company’s process and confirmed commercial starting points are now clear and visible in the top navigation. | The operational evidence pages that demonstrate this process should now follow. |
| Journal with 21 English articles | The site has a credible content engine and GitHub-to-Cloudflare publishing works automatically. | Only one English article family currently has Spanish and Russian editions; multilingual article depth is the largest content gap. |
| Founder/location positioning | Shenzhen, nearby factory regions, genuine services, and limits are consistently represented. | Trust would improve with authentic first-party evidence, not generic marketing claims. |

## Priority 1: build the Supplier Decision Suite

This is the next build I recommend. It creates four substantial page families, each in English, Spanish, Portuguese, Russian, Turkish, and French. That is **24 substantive non-blog routes** with no thin or duplicated filler.

| Page family | Buyer question | Essential sections | Main internal links |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Supplier verification** | “What can I check before I pay a deposit, and what does each check not prove?” | Identity and role; business information; quote consistency; evidence ladder; red flags; limits; next decision. | Supplier sourcing, Questions, factory visits, verification Journal article. |
| **Product samples and approval** | “How do I use a sample as a decision point, not just a package I receive?” | Sample types; sample brief; review sheet; revision record; approval boundaries; production handover. | Product Brief Builder, product guides, quality-control planning, sample articles. |
| **Factory visits** | “When is an in-person visit useful, what should I ask, and what should I take away?” | When to travel; visit agenda; production-flow questions; document/photo record; factory-visit limitations; travel planning. | China Route Map, city guides, factory-check service, Fees & scope. |
| **Quality-control planning** | “What must be agreed before production and before shipment?” | Approved reference; defect priorities; checkpoint timing; packaging checks; report logic; what inspection cannot guarantee. | Samples, quality-control service, shipping readiness, Questions. |

Each page should follow the same high-trust format:

> **What this checks → What it does not establish → What information you need → What decision comes next.**

This structure is better than claiming that a visit, sample, or check makes a supplier “safe.” The U.S. International Trade Administration frames due diligence as an ongoing process, while buyer-facing audit and inspection guides show the value of distinguishing scope, evidence, limitations, and the next action. ## Priority 2: add a real proof layer

The site should feel professional because it documents how work is done, not because it makes unverifiable claims. I recommend three first-party evidence pages after the Supplier Decision Suite.

| Page | What it would show | What I need from you |
|---|---|---|
| **What a factory-visit record can include** | A real example of agenda items, observed details, photo log structure, and open questions. It should be clearly labelled as an example, not a client result. | A redacted visit agenda or permission to create a neutral template from your actual working method. |
| **What a quality-check brief can include** | A buyer-friendly checklist: product version, sample, measurements, functional tests, packaging, defects, and decision owner. | A redacted checklist, or confirmation of the categories you normally check. |
| **Meet Azar / on-the-ground coverage** | Your genuine Shenzhen base, working languages, travel range, values, and contact preferences. | 8–15 original photos: you at work, city/market/factory exterior context, travel, product or packaging work. Faces, supplier names, labels, and sensitive material can be blurred. |

This is the most credible way to create a premium trust signal. **Do not add fake case studies, testimonials, client logos, “years of experience,” or audit certifications.** Authentic process evidence is more durable and easier to defend.

## Priority 3: complete operational and geographic decision paths

After the first four decision pages, build the next two route families:

| Page family | Why it matters | Connected routes |
|---|---|---|
| **Shipping readiness** | It connects product, approved sample, packaging, inspection, freight forwarder, documents, and handover responsibilities. This is a natural late-stage page for buyers who are nearly ready to ship. | Freight-forwarder Journal article, shipping guide, packaging, quality control, Fees & scope. |
| **China business travel** | It turns the existing city content and China Route Map into a clear buyer itinerary decision page. | China Route Map, Shenzhen/Guangzhou/Foshan/Dongguan/Zhongshan/Huizhou city pages, factory visits, independent travel support. |

The full decision suite would make the site feel materially larger because each page has a different job in the buyer’s project. It also creates a strong internal-link loop: product idea → supplier → sample → quality → shipping, with China travel as an optional evidence route.

## Priority 4: expand multilingual Journal depth deliberately

The Journal should not be translated randomly. First complete the Spanish and Russian editions of the pages closest to a sourcing decision, then publish Portuguese, Turkish, and French editions in the same order.

| First translation group | Why it comes first |
|---|---|
| Supplier comparison; supplier verification; product samples; quality-control checklist | These articles support the four new decision pages and reinforce internal links. |
| First import; Incoterms; freight forwarder; shipping planning | These support buyers approaching the shipment stage. |
| MOQ; packaging; Chinese holiday planning; supplier communication | These help visitors compare supplier responses and plan a project responsibly. |

The existing shared `translationKey` workflow is ready for this. Every completed edition should be reviewed for local phrasing and internal links before it is published; availability panels remain preferable to showing English copy on a non-English route.

## Priority 5: measure before adding a large second wave

Once the first decision suite is live, install or verify analytics and search measurement before adding dozens more pages. The useful questions are: which language markets enter through which page family; whether visitors progress from a guide into a Project Brief or Service Matcher; which internal links are used; and which pages create a WhatsApp or email conversation. This is not a request for a form or a sales funnel—Sourcing Ally can preserve its email-and-WhatsApp-only contact model.

A lightweight measurement setup should track outbound WhatsApp and email clicks, Project Brief Builder completion, Service Matcher completion, language selection, and the first page in a visit. It should be paired with a clear privacy notice and only implemented in a way you are comfortable with.

| **Batch 1 — next** | Supplier verification, product samples, factory visits, and quality-control planning in six languages. | Highest buyer value; fills the key pre-order decision gap; adds 24 meaningful non-blog routes. |

| **Batch 5** | Measurement review, then next product and regional page families based on real visitor paths. | Prevents the website from becoming large but unfocused. |

## What I recommend starting now

I recommend **Batch 1: the Supplier Decision Suite**. It is the best answer to your concern that the non-blog website still feels sparse. It increases depth, visible structure, menu discovery, SEO/GEO internal linking, and buyer usefulness at the same time.

The only later material I will need from you is authentic evidence for Batch 2. You do not need to send it now. Whenever you are ready, original work photos and a redacted real checklist or visit outline will let the site become significantly more personal and credible.
