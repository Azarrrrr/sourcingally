## Evidence boundaries

The purchase-order article will treat a purchase order, quote, pro forma invoice, sample record, specification, and payment decision as related but distinct records. It will not claim that one document guarantees performance or replaces legal or commercial review. The article can responsibly explain that product description, quantity, price, weight, specifications, and delivery basis must be consistent across commercial preparation, while destination requirements vary.[1]

The packaging article will distinguish selling-unit packaging, master cartons, labels, inserts, packing data, and shipment marks. It will advise buyers to confirm destination-market requirements with the relevant importer or qualified professional, rather than presenting a universal label or customs checklist. The article will use packaging as a production-control subject, not as destination-specific compliance advice.

The supplier-change article will use a simple documented-change workflow. It will distinguish a request, a proposed change, a confirmed change, a revised sample, and a commercial approval. It will not claim that documentation prevents all production problems; the buyer keeps the commercial approval decision.

## Source-grounded facts

The U.S. International Trade Administration describes a pro forma invoice as a pre-shipment negotiating document and notes that a commercial invoice and packing list contain different but related shipment information. Its guidance emphasizes that import-country requirements vary and that the buyer/importer or forwarder should confirm the required documents for the actual shipment.[1]

The International Trade Administration explains that Incoterms allocate specified tasks, costs, and risks, but do not identify the goods, list the contract price, set payment timing, pass title, or settle all other contract terms.[2] The ICC describes Incoterms® 2020 as a globally used framework that allocates cost, risk, and obligations; the new articles will therefore never treat an Incoterm alone as a complete purchase order or shipping plan.[3]
