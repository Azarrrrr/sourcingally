# Research notes: freight forwarder article

## Sources reviewed

1. U.S. Bureau of Industry and Security, [Freight forwarder guidance and best practices](https://www.bis.gov/freight-forward-guidance).
2. USPS Delivers, [Freight Forwarding](https://www.uspsdelivers.com/freight-forwarding/).

## Key findings to preserve accurately

A freight forwarder’s core role is to arrange and manage movement of cargo, not to replace the importer, exporter, or product owner’s own responsibilities. USPS describes forwarders as agents that organize international shipments for importing and exporting companies; available services can include ocean or air movement, inland transport, consolidation, warehousing, export-compliant packing, and customs-related preparation.

The BIS guidance is specific to U.S. export-control contexts and should not be presented as a universal rule for every China-origin shipment. It is useful for the broader principle that responsibilities must be documented, communication must be clear, information should be complete and accurate, and parties should resolve document discrepancies. Buyers must confirm responsibilities and current regulatory requirements for their actual product, route, Incoterm, exporter, importer, and destination.

The planned Journal article will avoid claims that a forwarder automatically clears customs, handles all compliance, or assumes the buyer’s importer obligations. It will advise readers to agree in writing on scope, handover point, documents, responsibilities, cutoffs, fees, and escalation contacts.
