# Sourcing Ally field-evidence intake checklist

## Purpose

This checklist helps Sourcing Ally turn real sourcing work into credible website evidence without inventing claims, exposing client information, or promising outcomes that cannot be guaranteed. The strongest future content will show **how decisions were handled**, not make vague claims about client volume, experience, or success rates.

## What can be shared safely

| Evidence type | Useful details to provide | Remove or confirm before publishing |
|---|---|---|
| Factory or supplier visit | General city/area, month and year, visit objective, agenda, what was observed, questions raised, next step | Client name, supplier name, exact address, confidential pricing, personal contacts, any unverified conclusion. |
| Sample review | Product category, version difference, checks performed, what changed, decision made, anonymized photographs | Brand identifiers, proprietary drawings, client communications, product secrets, test results not actually performed. |
| Packaging coordination | General packaging problem, carton/protection issue, artwork-proof question, agreed change, lessons learned | Brand files, barcodes, supplier pricing, unapproved client materials. |
| Production or final check | Agreed scope, visible requirements checked, evidence format, issue escalation process, decision needed | A promise that all defects were found, pass/fail guarantees, confidential quantities or factory identity. |
| China route or market note | Place visited, practical travel/logistics observation, what a buyer should prepare, linked product category | Statements that every supplier in a region is suitable, personal data, restricted areas, unsupported claims. |
| Founder profile | Actual working languages, Shenzhen base, travel coverage, practical working method, editorial review approach | Unverified years of experience, number of clients, certifications, office/warehouse claims, testimonials. |

## Minimum package for one field note

A field note does not need to be long. Please provide the following in English, Spanish, Russian, or simple notes—Sourcing Ally can shape the final article later.

1. **Where and when:** The city or general area plus approximate month/year.
2. **Why the work happened:** For example, sample review, supplier conversation, factory visit, packaging clarification, or quality question.
3. **What was checked:** List up to five facts or questions that were actually reviewed.
4. **What became clearer:** Describe the next decision, not a success claim.
5. **Photographs:** Two to six original images if you have permission to use them. Phone images are fine.
6. **What must stay private:** Names, brands, product designs, factory information, prices, or any fact that should not be mentioned.

## Suggested photo list

| Photo | Why it helps | Safety check |
|---|---|---|
| A travel or city context image | Gives the article a real Shenzhen, Guangdong, Yiwu, or market setting | Avoid faces and private information without consent. |
| A general factory exterior or visit moment | Shows real on-the-ground work | Use only with permission; do not show exact location if confidential. |
| A neutral process image | Supports sample, packaging, or quality content | Remove labels, client brands, QR codes, addresses, and sensitive paperwork. |
| A comparison detail | Makes an article useful: carton corner, sample finish, measurement method, packaging detail | Only if the product and brand can be shown. |
| A working-notes image | Supports the decision framework | Blur names, phone numbers, quotation values, and confidential drawings. |

## Publication rule

Every future field note should explain its limits. It can say what was seen or checked on that occasion, but it should not claim that a supplier is permanently reliable, that a visit guarantees future production quality, or that the same approach fits every product or destination.

## How to send material

Send a folder or message containing the notes and original photographs. State clearly: **“Okay to publish”**, **“Use only without names”**, or **“Private—use only to improve general educational content.”**
