## A purchase order is not a magic document

A purchase order can make an order clearer. It cannot, by itself, prove that a supplier understands every technical detail, that a sample represents future production, or that a shipment will arrive without a problem. Its real value is more practical: it gives the buyer and supplier one written record of the version being ordered.

Before a deposit, the important question is not simply, “Do we have a PO?” It is, “Can someone who did not follow the chat history identify the exact product, quantity, price basis, packaging, timing, delivery term, and open decisions?” If the answer is no, the order is not yet easy to control.

## Put the core order facts in one place

A clean order record should connect the quotation, product brief, approved sample, artwork, payment basis, and shipping handover. The format can be a purchase order, a supplier confirmation, or a combined record. What matters is that the same version is visible to both sides.

| Item to confirm | What should be clear in writing |
| --- | --- |
| Product version | Product name, photos, drawing or file reference, revision date, material, dimensions, colour, function, and included components. |
| Quantity | Total order quantity, variations, size or colour split, MOQ assumptions, and permitted production tolerance if one is agreed. |
| Price basis | Currency, unit price, total price, tooling or sample charges, packaging cost, and what is excluded. |
| Packaging | Selling-unit packaging, labels, inserts, master carton assumptions, shipping marks, and any approved artwork file. |
| Timing | What starts the lead time, what event means goods are ready, and which dates are estimates or commitments. |
| Payment | Deposit amount, balance trigger, receiving entity, bank-detail confirmation process, and documents expected before the next payment. |
| Quality reference | Approved sample, specification, measurable requirements, agreed checks, and who must confirm a change. |
| Delivery basis | The agreed Incoterms® rule, named place or port, version of the rule, and the forwarder or importer questions still open. |

Do not use the table as a legal template. Use it as a way to find ambiguity before payment. A simple line such as “blue box included” is not enough if the product has multiple box sizes, a barcode, an insert, a master carton requirement, or a destination-market label question.

## Make the commercial records agree

A quotation, pro forma invoice, purchase order, commercial invoice, and packing list do different jobs. They should not contradict each other. The U.S. International Trade Administration describes a pro forma invoice as an early transaction document and notes that a commercial invoice and packing list carry related but different shipment information.[1]

At the order stage, compare the following before you send money:

1. Does the product description match the sample or specification?
2. Do the quantity, price, currency, and packaging assumptions match the quote?
3. Does the receiving company and bank information match the company you expect to contract with?
4. Does the delivery term name the actual place or port, rather than only saying “FOB China” or “DDP”? 
5. Is there a written record of what still needs approval before production or shipment?

An Incoterm allocates specified tasks, costs, and risk. It does not identify the product, set payment timing, transfer ownership, or solve every contract question.[2] Treat the delivery term as one line in a wider order record, not as the whole agreement.

## Decide what a deposit actually releases

Before the deposit, ask the supplier to confirm what it will start after payment. Is it material purchase, tooling, sample revision, artwork proofing, production scheduling, or full production? The answer affects what you need approved first.

For a customized product, it is often sensible to keep a short “not yet approved” list. It might include final artwork, carton dimensions, a colour reference, a test question, an inspection date, or a shipping-mark format. A visible open item is safer than an assumption hidden in a chat message.

## A last check before you send money

Read the final record once as if you were the person receiving the goods. If you could not identify the product version, payment basis, package, delivery handover, and next evidence step, ask for clarification. That is not mistrust; it is basic order control.
